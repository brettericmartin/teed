Short version: a strip recessed into turf over foam tiles suits a bay that never moves; a full-stance mat suits a room that has to change back. The cost people forget is the replacement strip, because the strike zone is the only part that wears out.
</Callout>

## Who this is for

You have picked the enclosure and the launch monitor, and the mat is the last line on the list with the least money left for it. Or you built the bay a year ago and your lead elbow has started to ache. Either way the room is probably a garage or basement bay around 10 ft wide and 5 ft deep on concrete, and the launch monitor is already chosen, which decides more about the mat than most guides admit.

## Why the mat matters more than the screen

The screen takes the ball. The mat takes you. Every swing into a hard surface sends shock up the shaft into the wrists and lead elbow, and the forums repeat one story: cheap mat, a month of enthusiasm, tendonitis. Friendly Golfer's roundup opens with tennis elbow after 30 days on a cheap mat. A Slickdeals thread adds the correction worth remembering: thickness alone is not protection. A thick commercial range mat still wrecked one poster's joints; a bristle mat did not. Golf Simulator Forum's budget thread states the rule: preventing injury outweighs realism, every time. Landing and surround turf are a separate guide.

## The sub-shapes

### Full-stance

One surface, 4 x 5 to 6 x 10 ft, roll it out and hit. It suits a room that has to change back into a garage. About $400 for a 4 x 4 to $1,400 for a 4 x 10. The trade-off: it wears as one piece.

### Strip

A 12 x 28 to 12 x 46.5 in panel dropped into DIY turf over 1 in foam tiles, for a permanent bay with a built-up floor. $75 to $280, stance turf extra. The trade-off is the flush cut: measure every layer, because a strip that sits high changes your posture and one that sits low catches the club (The Garage Golfer and Carl's flooring guide both lead with this).

### Insert system

A full-stance mat with a replaceable hitting panel: 1 x 4 or 2 x 4 ft in the Fiberbuilt studio mats, 30 x 12 in in Carl's HotShot, 28 x 12 in in the SIGPRO. One mat, and an $80 to $280 fix when the center wears. $500 to $1,300. The trade-off is weight and seams.

### Launch-monitor-friendly

A fit, not a construction: side cameras sit level with the ball, overhead cameras need the panel to cover their zone, radar wants a level run to the screen. Mats sold on this carry a 2 ft spacer panel or a 2 x 4 ft strike panel, $1,300 to $1,500, and are built for a bay that never moves.

### Practice-portable

Ovals and small three-zone mats, $30 to $500, for a net or a launch monitor on the lawn. None is a stance surface on its own; the ball sits above your feet.

## What the forum argues about

**Feel or forgiveness.** Fiberbuilt Grass and the Softy win the joint side; Player Preferred, CCE and the HotShot gel win the realism side. The fat shot shows it: the Grass lets it through, the Player Preferred punishes it, the CCE slows it like turf, the Softy may under-punish it, the Bullseye mimics it. The GolfWRX thread on using both the Softy and a Fiberbuilt splits the same way (search snippets, unverified). Decide whether you want feedback or want to keep playing, then buy for that.

**The Reddit survey.** No reddit.com thread surfaced for any mat query we ran on October 19, 2026. The only Reddit signal is secondhand: Yardstick Golf's March 2026 summary of a survey of just over 300 Reddit buyers, scoring Fiberbuilt top for shock absorption, Bullseye top for feel, CCE below average on both. One self-selected survey, reported by a third party. Read r/Golfsimulator yourself before treating it as a verdict.

**Strip or full mat.** Golf Simulator Forum's rule: stance mat firm so your feet do not squish, strip compliant so your wrists survive. Sweepers can use one CCE surface; divot takers want a firm stance and a soft strip. A $180 to $280 strip replaces the only part that wears; a $500 to $1,300 mat wears as one piece. The other half of the argument is strip size against the camera, covered below; one member also reports only about 12 x 18 in of true strip on some Player Preferred units.

**Mat height.** 1 1/8 in (EZ Tee) to 2.5 in (Softy). The 1.75 in group (HotShot, Player Preferred, CCE, SwingTurf) lines up with 1 in foam tiles plus turf; the 2 to 2.5 in group (Fiberbuilt Grass, Softy, TrueStrike) needs about a 2 in subfloor to putt flush and a riser for a side camera. MyGolfSpy flagged the Softy's height for low ceilings.

**The turf-color anecdote.** One GC3 owner in the 2023 Bullseye thread read about half his shots on the bright-green strip and near-perfect on a solid olive mat, with no fix posted. MyGolfSim's guide adds that busy turf patterns under the ball add noise to camera reads. That is one anecdote and one guide line, not a rule; no source says dark turf is required, and we found nothing on lie boards.

## Launch-monitor fit

Side cameras (SkyTrak, GC3, Launch Pro, EYE MINI) must sit level with the ball: a mat wide enough to hold the unit (the Fiberbuilt studio mats' 2 ft spacer panel, SIGPRO's strip extensions) or a riser beside a tall mat. Friendly Golfer could not swing a driver with a GCQuad on a TrueStrike Single.

Overhead units (GCQuad, EYE XO, EYE XO2) need the strip to cover the hitting zone: the GCQuad about 14 x 14 in, the EYE XO about 12 x 16 in. The Softy's 12 x 28 in strip covers a GC3 (about 7 x 10 in) but not a GCQuad; the Player Preferred 8 x 4's 2 x 4 ft panel covers both. Uneekor lists the EYE XO, EYE MINI and QED as not compatible with trouble mats and ships the EZ Tee Hybrid in its EYE XO SIMKIT. Carl's sells black tees so tee color does not interfere with ball reads, and notes that tee-receiver holes can fall outside a monitor's zone.

Radar (Mevo+, R10, Trackman) does not care about mat height but wants a level run from ball to screen. Top Shelf Golf adds the step everyone skips: recalibrate once the mat is down.

## If the bay has to fold away

In a fold-flat bay the enclosure goes against the wall and the mat is the one part that never folds; it is what is still on the floor when the car wants in. Weight decides it. A 21 lb SIGPRO 4 x 7 snaps apart and slides aside; a HotShot ships rolled and rolls again; the Fiberbuilt studio mats at 122 to 141 lb and the TrueStrike Academy at 134 lb are furniture. For a net-only setup the 69 lb Practice Station is the portable end. StowGolf's published bay is 10 ft wide and 5 ft deep, 7 ft 6 in to the screen top, freestanding, and folds flat against the wall (list $2,495; the first 25 at $2,295; a $100 refundable reservation holds a numbered slot). Most full mats are 4 ft deep, so a 5 ft deep bay keeps about a foot behind the tee; 5 ft deep mats (CCE and HotShot 5 x 8, SwingTurf 5 x 5) fill it edge to edge.

## The costs nobody prints

The subfloor: 1 in foam tiles under stance turf for a strip (Carl's says 1/2 to 1 in), about a 2 in build-up for a 2 to 2.5 in mat to putt flush, and Golf Simulator Depot's 3/8 in underlay even under an EZ Tee on concrete.

The base: the HotShot's is sold separately and nearly doubles the cost (Golfer Geeks); the Holy Grail and EZ Tee need velcro or glue.

The replacement strip, which sets the five-year price: SIGPRO strip $249.99 and Holy Grail $179.99 (October 19, 2026); HotShot inserts about $80 to $280; Fiberbuilt Grass strip about $279 on the comparison charts; a $600 quote for a Player Preferred section (unverified); TrueStrike surface $55 to $99 and gel plus surface about $385, older quotes with parts availability questioned. A fair five-year number is the mat plus two strips.

Tees: CCE, SwingTurf, EZ Tee, Bullseye, Softy and the HotShot standard take a wooden tee; the Holy Grail, HotShot foam and gel, Softy LITE and SIGPRO 3D do not. Real tees fly around indoors, so many owners buy cone tees anyway.

The second mat: the CCE refugees on every thread paid twice. SwingTurf's lifetime warranty extension is $89. TrueStrike was out of stock or backordered at both US retailers on October 19, 2026, and the Fiberbuilt adjustable tee is out of production. And once the mat is down, the launch monitor needs recalibrating, which costs an evening.
