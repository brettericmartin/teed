<Callout variant="info">
Short version: if the car still parks in the bay, the shape of the enclosure matters more than the brand on it. A net cage or a retractable gives the floor back, a fixed frame takes it for good, and the cost nobody prints is the second afternoon spent loosening bungees after the first screen sends a ball back at your head.
</Callout>

## Who this is for

You have a garage, or a basement with a ceiling between 8 and 9 ft, and a launch monitor already picked. You know the two numbers the category lives between: one Golf Simulator Forum builder found that "$5,000 appeared to be the average price" for an enclosure and bought a $414 Walmart cage instead; another put a Carl's Place kit together with $106 of hardware-store conduit for $1,006. Everything below sits in that spread, and the question it is built around is the forum's: what happens to the floor when the car comes back.

## The five shapes

### Net cage

A practice net with a ball return. No projection unless you add a target sheet, so it is a hitting bay first. 3.5 to 8 ft deep, up in minutes, down into a bag. Price band $240 to $795. Floor space when the car comes back: none. The trade: you buy a screen later, or never.

### Freestanding fixed frame or kit

A pipe or aluminum frame with an impact screen and a surround, up for good. This is most of the market. Price band $500 to $5,600. Footprint 4 ft 2 in to 6 ft deep by 8 to 12 ft wide, and the car does not come back to that bay. The trade: the steadiest image once tuned, and a frame height of 7 ft 6 in to 9 ft 4 in that decides whether your ceiling works at all.

### Retractable

A roller that lifts the screen, and sometimes the sides, to the ceiling or the wall. Price band $1,999 to $4,500, or $30 to $599 for the curtain-track versions people build. The housing stays overhead, the floor comes back, the car parks. The trade: a screen that hangs instead of stretches, so owners report wrinkles, a loose bottom edge and a softer picture, plus hours of hanging things from joists.

### DIY

EMT conduit and canopy fittings, a kit or a bare screen, curtain track, or hinges. Price band $500 to about $1,263 as posted. Fixed unless you hinge it; the hinged builds rest at about 1 x 8 ft against a wall. The trade: a Saturday, a grinder, and the engineering is on you.

### Custom room

A builder designs and installs the room. Published starting points run from $34,995 to $100,000-plus, and the enclosure is a line item inside a launch-monitor-led package. Floor space when the car comes back: the room, permanently. (The inflatable is a sixth shape we treat as a footnote: a sealed-air PVC bay that deflates but wants 12 x 16 ft of floor while up; the All Sports 12 x 16 was $2,999 and sold out on October 19, 2026.)

### The shape the forum keeps asking for: freestanding fold-flat

A frame that folds against the wall without being bolted to anything. The forum has wanted this for years: threads from 2017, 2020 and 2023 pivoted, hinged and asked for one, and all three still get bumped. The products that claim a fold today are bolted to the wall, silent about the mount, or not yet shipping. Price band $900 to $2,495. Floor space when the car comes back: a strip along the wall.

## The DIY math

The forum's benchmark is a Carl's kit on self-cut EMT. In June 2019 a builder paid $899.95 for the 8 x 8 kit and $106.39 for nine sticks of 1 in EMT, $1,006.34 before a cutting tool, then spent 30 minutes with an angle grinder. In July 2020 another builder's custom 8.5 x 11 with pipe and cutter came to $1,263.04. Today the kit is $999.95 to $1,199.94, EMT is $8 to $12 a stick (Home Performance Lab, June 2026), and the $349.95 pipe kit is the price of skipping the hardware store, which will cut the sticks if you ask. The $500 blueprint (Sim Golf Lab, December 2025) frames 10 ft x 8 ft 6 in x 5 ft from 11 sticks at about $250, $30 of foam, canopy fittings that Home Depot does not stock, and a GoSports grommeted screen; the article says roughly $500, its priced lines add to about $290 and the screen is unpriced. Gung Ho Golf's 2023 16 x 9 ft build came to about $1,060 with a good screen. Yardstick (April 2026) puts the conduit for a 10 x 12 under $200 and repeats two rules: thumb-screw canopy fittings, never electrical elbows, and never PVC, which shatters. What none of them include: padding (foam lagging is the usual fix), a baffle, a warranty on the pipe, or your Saturday.

The fold-aways are where the want shows up in metal. Curtain track is the cheapest: a 2022 forum builder hung a grommeted Carl's screen from an Amazon room-divider track and had 18 months on it, and every reply in that thread is about anchoring the bottom with PVC posts and bungees to floor weights; Rain or Shine's [ceiling curtain-track kit](https://rainorshinegolf.com/products/diy-golf-simulator-enclosure-curtain-kit) is $599 base with no impact screen, and a YouTube build claims a $30 version. The 2020 thread hinged a Carl's enclosure's side panels on EMT to a resting footprint of about 1 x 8 ft; the motive quoted was a wife who "refuses to give up half of our 2-car garage for a toy". The 2017 thread hung a 12 x 9 ft frame from eye lag bolts on velcro straps and pivoted it onto bike hooks, 6 in under the garage door, in a 9 ft 6 in garage. The Smart Home Hookup (February 2023) hoisted a steel-tube screen frame on pillow-block bearings into a ceiling vaulted by cutting four trusses: $550 for frame, hoist and safety rail, $595 for a Carl's screen, $10,177 for the whole garage project.

## The 8-ft ceiling question

golfingsim's tier table (September 2026) is the clearest answer we found: under 8 ft, a wall-hung panel; 8 ft to 8 ft 6 in, a short or semi-retractable sub-8-ft frame, driver only for golfers under 5 ft 8 in; 9 ft, the standard 8 ft 4 in frame; 10 ft, a Pro frame. Add a 2 to 3 in buffer over the frame and subtract the 2 to 2.5 in a mat takes. The Indoor Golf Shop's garage guide advises against building under 9 ft at all. The forum's 8 ft 4 in garage (thread 321467) hit a beam with a 3-wood, moved the hitting zone to 9 ft from the screen, and then cleared driver.

The frame heights in this research, per their makers: PlayBetter's SimStudio8 at 7 ft 6 in (on our watchlist with two sources) and the fold-flat frame in the section above at 7 ft 6 in to the screen top are the only ones under 8 ft; GoSports is 8 ft; GolfBays 8 ft 2 in; SIG8, SIG10 and the Carl's 8-ft kits 8 ft 4 in; HomeCourse wants 8 ft 6 in of ceiling, and SportScreen does not state one. Measure to the lowest obstruction, the opener, the rail, the beam, not the drywall (golfsimspot).

## What the forum argues about

Bounce-back is an enclosure problem, not a screen problem, and the fix list is the same for every shape. A Carl's DIY owner saw balls come back to head height from 10 ft and two hit his GC2 (thread 253998); the answer was to loosen the bungees, trim any bar that runs 1/2 to 3/4 in long, put a soft floor under the mat and hang foam behind the screen. Wedges come back over your head off the top third; thread 264435 settled on foam behind the top half and extra top bungees, with 12 in baffles spaced 16 in apart as the other route. Friendly Golfer's line stands: perfectly flat screens are not the goal.

Side gaps are the quiet argument. The full frames close the sides: Friendly Golfer found no gap behind the SIG10 even with inches of wall clearance. The retractables do not: HomeCourse is an 8 x 8 screen, not a bay, so tight rooms add netting or wall padding; G-TRAK gives under 3 ft of side netting; a curtain-track screen has no containment until you hang curtains and a baffle. The ball finds whatever is open.

"Folds" is doing a lot of work on product pages. The Net Return and Spornia fold into a bag. GolfBays says the SimBox "folds down for storage" and publishes no folded size, so treat it as a knock-down frame. Elite Screens' GolfSim Portable has a folding frame and a 44-lb box. Anything Sports "folds nearly flat" against the wall it is bolted to. Impact Sports "folds to 13 in width" and does not say what holds it up. HomeCourse, Vanish and G-TRAK retract rather than fold. When a page says "folds", the forum's follow-up is the right one: folded to what, and attached to what. Retractable sag is the related trade: HomeCourse owners give up image quality and a tight bottom edge for the remote; Vanish owners give up install time and money for a full bay that rolls up; curtain-track builders give up everything for $30 to $599 and a weekend of anchoring.

## The costs nobody prints

The pipe: Carl's DIY price is the kit, and the EMT is $106 to $470 more, plus a tube cutter or a grinder. The upgrades that make the cheap price quiet: SwingBay's triple-layer screen is $500, the Net Return's Simulator Kit is $399, Spornia's target sheet is sold separately, HomeCourse's wall kit is $79.99 to $125. The padding and the dark: foam lagging ($30 to $60 in the DIY builds), a blackout panel (about $100), wall padding at $300 to $1,200 if a contractor does it. The projector it forces: Golfstead puts 3,000-plus lumens behind a SwingBay. The floor beyond the frame: 11 x 15 ft for a SwingBay, 16 ft 9 in of depth for a default PerfectBay and 18 ft 9 in to swing a driver. The ceiling: 2 to 3 in of buffer above the frame and 2 to 2.5 in of mat below you. And the second afternoon: nearly every owner report above ends with bungees coming off, foam going in, or a baffle going up.
