## Who this is for

You have a bay about 10 ft wide, a 4:3 impact screen, a ceiling between 8 and 9 ft, and a ball 10 to 11 ft from the screen. You are choosing between a sub-$1,000 lamp unit and a $2,000 to $2,900 laser, you want to know where it hangs before you drill, and there is a fair chance the spot over the ball is already taken by a door track, a duct or a launch monitor. That case gets its own section. Screen, enclosure and computer are their own guides.

## Throw math for a 10 ft wide bay

Throw ratio is lens-to-screen distance divided by image width. Every projector here is a 16:9 panel and the screen is 4:3, so to put 7 ft 6 in of height on the fabric the 16:9 image has to be 13 ft 4 in wide, with the extra width masked in black. Distance is throw ratio times 13.33 ft, not times 10 (BenQ writes it as throw ratio = distance x 0.5625 / image height). For a 10 ft by 7 ft 6 in screen:

- 0.5 (Optoma's GT line, BenQ AH500ST and LK830ST): about 6 ft 7 in from the screen, ahead of a golfer standing 10 to 11 ft back, so no shadow is possible. Suits bays under 14 to 16 ft deep and low ceilings.
- 0.69 to 0.83 (BenQ TH671ST, TK710STi, AH700ST, AK700ST): 9 ft 2 in to 11 ft 1 in, above or just behind the ball. The LK936ST's 0.81 to 0.89 lands at 10 ft 10 in to 11 ft 10 in.
- 1.0: 13 ft 4 in, behind the golfer, where the shadow complaints start. The TK700STi (0.9 to 1.08) wants 12 ft to 14 ft 5 in, the Panasonic PT-VMZ51 13 to 21 ft, the ViewSonic PX701-4K 20 to 22 ft.
- Under 0.4 (ultra short throw): on the floor under the screen, where the balls land.

A 9 ft 6 in wide screen scales everything by 0.95. The forum's shadow check: ball distance from the screen is roughly your height divided by the lens height, times the lens-to-screen distance. Before drilling, hold the projector up on a ladder at the planned spot and swing; the July 2020 "Not much love for floor mounting" thread calls that the test that settles it.

## 4:3 vs 16:9 on an impact screen

Screens in bays under about 13 ft wide are 4:3 because width is the limit and height keeps a thinned wedge on the fabric; MyGolfSimulator warns a 16:9 image on a narrow screen looks too short. Letterboxing a 16:9 panel to 4:3 wastes 25 percent of the pixels and about a third of the light; ProjectorCentral's rule is to multiply the lumens you need by 1.33. BenQ's golf line handles the crop with Auto Screen Fit or Screen Fill, which reads the screen's black border (it needs 2.5 in of it) and fills 4:3 by itself. Everything else does it from the aspect menu, a few remote clicks per the TK710STi owners on Reddit, or by running the PC at 1440x1080 as the January 2021 EH412ST floor thread does.

## Ceiling, floor or cart

Ceiling, centred, behind the ball is the default; Carl's Place puts it about 4 ft behind the tee, and Top Shelf Golf lists ceiling mounts at $99. Optoma's GT units throw with a 116 percent upward offset, so flush to an 8 ft ceiling they need tilt; the one published comparison has the GT2100HDR making a 6.9 ft tall image from 8 ft up against 7.2 ft for the BenQ AH500ST.

Floor is the low-ceiling answer and only a 0.5 unit fits it, because the image projects upward from a low lens. The reference build is the December 2020 "Floor vs ceiling mount projector" thread: 8 ft 8 in ceiling, an Optoma EH412ST in a floor case 12 in off the hitting line and 7 ft 6 in from the screen, keystone at -6, rear feet shimmed on wood. Carl's Place says a floor mount needs a case and wedges; Top Shelf Golf's ProGuard floor mount is $349. The costs: heat in the case, a blocked putting line, a box in some golfers' follow-through.

Carts are the temporary-bay answer. Carl's Place calls them technically fine and realistically not (space, shadows, bumps, looks temporary), and ProjectorCentral adds that a cart beside the mat is hard to square because short throws have no horizontal shift.

## Off-centre mounting

This is the section the forum keeps writing. Every guide we read (Carl's Place, Home Performance Lab, MyGolfSimulator, Rain or Shine) gives the same order: physical placement first, optical lens shift second, digital keystone last and small, under 15 degrees per Rain or Shine.

Lens shift moves the whole image by moving the lens, so every pixel still lands and the picture stays rectangular. Keystone fakes it: the projector squeezes a trapezoid into a smaller rectangle, which discards pixels, softens the image, drops brightness on the corrected side and on some models disables the high refresh modes. BenQ's own explainer admits it adds input lag.

Among the home-tier models the sim community discusses, exactly two have lens shift. The BenQ LK936ST shifts +/-23 percent horizontally and +/-60 percent vertically; Home Performance Lab names it as the only unit for side mounting. The Panasonic PT-VMZ51 shifts +/-20 percent horizontally and +44 percent vertically, but it is standard throw and needs 13 ft or more. MyGolfSimulator's keystone page lists only the LK936ST and the Epson PowerLite L790SE ($4,600 to $6,100, commercial) with shift; Top Shelf Golf's table adds the BenQ AK900ST and LU935ST. On the watch list with fewer than five sources: the LG ProBeam BU53RG (H +/-20 percent, V +/-50 percent) and the Eiki EK-308U that Foresight stood to the golfer's right in Sim in a Box with about 7 percent of shift.

Keystone range is what everyone else has, and it is not equal. Optoma's GT line corrects +/-15 degrees per axis. BenQ's TK710STi and AH500ST do +/-30, the TK710STi on three axes. The Optoma UHD35STx and ViewSonic PX701-4K do +/-40, the ViewSonic with four-corner correction. The BenQ TH671ST and the old Optoma GT1080HDR correct vertically only and cannot be squared from beside the mat at all. The August 2024 GSF 4K thread warns that cheaper units distort visibly once pushed.

What builders do when the centre spot is gone:

- Over the ball, angled in. The February 2021 "Horizontal lens shift" thread's garage had a side wall as the only mount and no budget projector that shifts a metre; the fix was to hang the unit above the ball line, angle it toward the screen, and square the rest with a little keystone.
- A 0.5 unit in a floor case beside the hitting line: 12 in off the line in the December 2020 build, 3 ft off-centre in one October 2022 plan. Optoma's upward offset and the low offset on the AH500ST and LK830ST are what make a floor lens work.
- A cart to one side with a wide-keystone model: the UHD35STx and PX701-4K's +/-40 degrees are the budget range that reaches it, at the sharpness cost above.
- Beside a launch monitor. The January 2022 Eye XO thread stacks a TK700STi with its lens 1 to 2 in below the overhead unit and says to draw a side view at your own height first. When the monitor owns the centreline, the LK936ST's vertical shift is the clean fix.

## Real lumens in golf mode

Two testers have published measurements, and they agree with each other more than with the boxes. The Hook Up (August 2024) measured the BenQ TK710STi at 2,448 lumens in Living Room mode against a 3,200 rating and the AH700ST at 2,200 in Golf mode against 4,000. Projector Reviews (March 2025) measured the same AH700ST at 3,812 in Bright and about 2,287 in the other presets. ProjectorCentral got 2,582 from the ViewSonic PX701-4K against 3,200, and Home Performance Lab's 2026 figures (AK700ST 3,500 to 4,000 in accurate mode, TH671ST 2,000 to 2,500) are published as estimates. The rated figures only appear in Bright mode, which nobody plays in. Five r/Golfsimulator TK710STi owners say it plainly: great with the garage lights off, washed out with them on. Rain or Shine's targets run 2,500 for a basement to 4,000 plus for daylight, before the 1.33 letterbox factor. Blackout curtains are still the forum's cheapest lumen upgrade.

## What the forum argues about

4K or not. ProjectorCentral's guide says 4K shows less benefit on an impact screen, the LK936ST thread's original poster could not get guests to notice it, and Shop Indoor Golf calls it optional. The August 2024 thread is the counter: everyone building new wants it anyway, with the TK710STi as the price floor, and most then run 1080p/240 in GSPro for the lag.

BenQ or Optoma. Top Shelf Golf's split holds in the threads: Optoma for brightness per dollar and the 0.5 throw, BenQ for color accuracy, golf modes and 4K.

Is the LK936ST worth it. The thread title says no; later posters in the same thread say yes. Nobody buys it for picture per dollar; they buy it because the centreline is taken or the lights stay on.

Floor mounting. "Not much love for floor mounting" (July 2020) is still the reference argument: in the follow-through, blocks putts, heat in the case. One owner's offset floor unit works fine, and the December 2020 build is the proof.

Lamp or laser. The TH671ST and TK700STi still sell on price. Every 2026 guide flags fading bulbs, $150 to $300 replacements getting scarce, and unsealed engines in dusty garages, which is what the Optoma IP6X and BenQ IP5X ratings are for.

## If the bay has to fold away

A fold-flat or roll-away bay changes the mount more than the projector. A ceiling mount in a garage that gets the car back is permanent hardware over a space that is empty most of the week, and a floor case sits where the car parks. What builders do is put the projector on the thing that leaves: a cart that rolls out with the bay, which turns Carl's Place's "temporary" objection into the point. The pairing is a 0.5 unit at about 6 ft 7 in from the screen, ahead of the golfer, plus a wide keystone range if the cart has to sit to one side of the hitting line. A shallow bay is also where the UST question comes from, and the forum answer still applies: the unit would sit where balls land.

A freestanding fold-flat enclosure such as StowGolf is one of the bays this applies to. Its published facts: 10 ft wide, 5 ft deep, 7 ft 6 in to the screen top, freestanding, folds flat against the wall; list $2,495, the first 25 at $2,295, a $100 refundable reservation holds a numbered slot. With 5 ft of depth, the ball and the projector both sit outside the enclosure footprint, so the cart rolls away with the mat; Brett's own garage build on Teed runs a TK710STi on a rolling cart for that reason. Disclosure: Brett Martin runs Teed and builds StowGolf.

## The costs nobody prints

The mount. $99 for a ceiling mount at Top Shelf Golf; $349 for the ProGuard floor case, plus wedges or shims.

The cable. A ceiling or floor unit sits 10 to 15 ft from the PC, so budget a long HDMI run; no source we read priced one.

The lamp. 4,000 hours in normal mode on the TH671ST and TK700STi, 6,000 on the PX701-4K, then $100 to $300 for a replacement that is getting harder to find. Lasers are rated 20,000 to 30,000 hours, which is most of the price gap.

The letterbox. Multiply the lumens you think you need by 1.33, then shop on measured figures, not rated ones.

The computer. 4K in GSPro wants an RTX 3060-class card or better per Home Performance Lab.

The second purchase. A TK700STi buyer in August 2022 was talked into a UHD35STx; the PX701-4K buyer in May 2021 ended up with a TH671ST. Draw the side view first.

The lens in the strike zone. Flop shots reach 55 degrees in the December 2018 VZ580 thread, so pad the beam near a ceiling lens.
